**The baby did not cry.**

Dr. Evelyn Hart has spent her career rewriting human biology at the molecular level. When her daughter Nunnally is born blind, limbless, and silent, Evelyn does what she has always done when the science fails and the ethics board isn't watching — she improvises. In secret, she injects the infant with an experimental mRNA vector. The clicking that begins inside Nunnally's limb nubs three days later is not a complication. It is an answer.

But Evelyn's husband Marcus carries a secret of his own. At a classified military facility thirty-seven miles south of their Arlington home, a teenage boy sits in permanent containment — not because he is dangerous in any conventional sense, but because his body produces a compound that rewrites human biology through proximity. Through touch. Through love. The compound doesn't kill its hosts. It recruits them. Everyone who gets close becomes dependent. Everyone who leaves begins to die. And Marcus, who stole three drops of Nunnally's blood to keep the boy alive, is already too deep to pull free.

When sixteen-year-old Elara Voss — who carries the compound in her own scars from a tragedy she barely survived — is drawn into the program's orbit, she discovers that the line between weapon and victim dissolved long ago. The boy is not a monster. He is lonely. And the compound spreading through the Hart family does not trap them — it makes the trap feel like home.

As Evelyn races to understand the connection between her daughter's transformation and the boy's biology, she confronts a truth more horrifying than any weapon: the most brilliant thing she has ever done is the thing that is destroying everyone she loves. And undoing it would mean unmaking her daughter.

WOMB OF SHADOWS is a 70,000-word dark literary sci-fi horror novel, the first in a planned trilogy.
