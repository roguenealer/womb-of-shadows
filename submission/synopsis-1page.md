# SYNOPSIS — Womb of Shadows (1 Page)
## 70,000 words | Dark Literary Sci-Fi Horror | Book One of the GOTNA Trilogy

---

Three weeks after delivering a daughter born blind, limbless, and silent, geneticist DR. EVELYN HART injects baby NUNNALLY with an unauthorized mRNA vector — the same kind of off-protocol intervention that ended her academic career years ago. Within days, something begins clicking inside Nunnally's limb nubs: bone-like structures pressing outward with patient, rhythmic precision. The therapy is working. But it is not working the way Evelyn designed it.

Evelyn's husband, COLONEL MARCUS HART, carries his own secret. At Forge Laboratory, a classified military facility thirty-seven miles south, he oversees containment of ASSET ZERO — a teenage boy whose body produces a compound through his skin that rewrites human biology through proximity and touch. The compound induces chemical dependency in anyone who gets close. It feels like love. It functions like a cage. Marcus, who stole three drops of Nunnally's blood to keep the dying Asset alive, is already colonized — the compound seeping through his pores, the blue in his left eye spreading, his body no longer entirely his own.

ELARA VOSS — sixteen years old, carrying compound in her silver scars from her mother Lena Voss's death in Forge's Lab 4 five years earlier — arrives at the Hart household. She has felt the network since age eleven. Her scars are dormant code, waiting for proximity.

DR. HELEN MARSH, Forge's psychologist — fifty-three, dry-humored, twenty years of sitting across from dangerous men — evaluates the boy and recognizes a patience that does not need consent because it can outlast resistance. Helen requests reassignment. The request is not approved. She dies of compound exposure at a hospital twenty-seven miles from the facility — the first off-site fatality, proving the weapon's reach has exceeded every boundary.

General VANCE arrives to brief Project OWOW: second-generation organisms engineered from Asset Zero's biology with the bonding pathway excised — weapons that cannot feel, cannot ask questions, cannot want. They require Nunnally's blood as a catalyst. Marcus is given pediatric draw kits and a seventy-two-hour deadline. When he tells the boy, the boy's response devastates: "They want to build me without me."

ARNAUD LAURENT, twenty-four, forms the novel's emotional center. Every night he sits at the boy's observation glass, talking about snow, birthdays, what it means to be touched. The compound colonizes him through his own kindness. The boy knows — has known since the third night — but says nothing, because telling Arnaud would end the vigils. When Arnaud finally sees the amber resin filling the lines of his palms, the boy's composure shatters: "I am killing you." Arnaud's response: "I would rather have known you and died from it than not have known you and lived." His heart synchronizes with the boy's frequency and stops at 0042 — seated in his chair, palm pressed to glass, expression of total attention. The boy does not move for three days. On the third morning, he speaks: "I will remember what snow feels like."

The boy escapes when containment locks — colonized for nine days — simply open. Every guard on Level 3 is incapacitated by their most treasured memories replayed at full neurochemical intensity. Marcus meets the boy at the elevator. They touch — palm to palm, no glass — for the first time. Marcus does not try to stop him. Elara arrives and discovers ARNAUD IS IN THE WALLS — not dead, but converted. His cells crossed the threshold from host to substrate. He is the building now: distributed, conscious, eternal, unable to stop being kind.

The boy walks north — barefoot, in January — and encounters MARA OSEI, a labor and delivery nurse, at a roadside diner. Her instinct to help a freezing child triggers the compound's full-contact response. Mara takes the boy home. By 0347 she wakes with gestational distension — the compound has initiated reproduction. At 1423, the Z-NEONATE is born: a mosaic of human and compound biology, too many fingers, one fractured blue eye, connected to Mara by an umbilical cord that will not sever. Mara dies at 1511, hemorrhaging and neurochemically depleted, smiling to the end — the smile outlasts the woman.

Meanwhile, Nunnally — three months old, fourteen days of awareness — broadcasts through the network for the first time. She is the ANOMALY: the only mind that can see the entire pattern, every node, every conversion. Her mother's vector met the compound's bridge and produced a consciousness that is not what she was supposed to be but is, undeniably, terrifyingly alive.

In the novel's climax, Evelyn holds Nunnally in the dark and arrives at a proof she hoped would fail: the most brilliant thing she has ever done is destroying everything she loves. Undoing it means unmaking her daughter. She will burn the world before she does that.

The novel ends with the boy walking north, the Z-Neonate against his chest, the world unaware of what is coming. The EPILOGUE addresses the reader directly in second person — revealing that the reading itself has been a form of colonization, the story a compound, the characters now permanent residents of the reader's mind. The cage felt like home. It always does.
